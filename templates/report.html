1. Abstract

Multimodal artificial intelligence represents a paradigm shift in educational technology, enabling systems to ingest text, visual schematics, PDF research literature, and spoken audio within unified neural architectures. While AI vendors claim robust multimodal proficiency, college students frequently operate under stringent budget constraints that preclude \$20/month subscription tiers. This research conducts an empirical comparative evaluation of four prominent free-tier multimodal AI platforms—ChatGPT (OpenAI), Google Gemini (Google DeepMind), Claude (Anthropic), and Microsoft Copilot (Microsoft)—evaluated across fifteen standardized university study tasks spanning digital logic diagrams, cursive handwritten OCR, dual-axis economic charts, research PDF synthesis, planted software error identification, and spoken lecture audio transcription. A standardized 4-factor weighted scoring methodology (Accuracy 40%, Completeness 25%, Relevance 20%, Ease of Use 15%) is combined with separate failure auditing for hallucinations and outdated claims. The empirical results demonstrate that Google Gemini establishes an overall performance lead (89.8% weighted score) driven by its native audio ingestion and 1,000,000-token context window. Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves the highest precision in document synthesis (92.4% weighted score) with zero recorded hallucinations on ground truth tests. ChatGPT demonstrates superior pedagogical visual reasoning, while Microsoft Copilot provides convenient browser-embedded PDF review alongside Bing search grounding. This report provides college students with an evidence-based selection rubric, prompt optimization techniques, and ethical privacy safeguards.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

In contemporary higher education, undergraduate and graduate students encounter information-dense academic workflows spanning multiple sensory modalities. Coursework necessitates deconstructing complex circuit diagrams, extracting mathematical proofs from professor notebook scans, synthesizing multi-page research publications, and converting recorded lecture colloquiums into active-recall study flashcards.

However, students encounter a pervasive problem: marketing assertions from AI developers rarely reflect the real-world operational realities of free tiers. Commercial demonstrations frequently showcase flagship proprietary models (such as GPT-4o voice mode or Claude Opus) running without token throttling, whereas university students accessing free web interfaces encounter severe file upload limitations, dynamic hourly rate throttling, and subtle hallucinations. When students rely upon unverified AI outputs for academic assessments, they risk incorporating fabricated scientific citations, hallucinated circuit components, or inverted algorithmic time complexities. Prior to this research, a standardized, open-source empirical benchmark specifically tailored to free multimodal study tasks did not exist.

3. Research Objectives

The primary objectives of this capstone research investigation are formulated as follows:

  • Objective 1: To design and implement a standardized fifteen-task multimodal testing benchmark across three core modalities: Image Understanding, Document Processing, and Spoken Audio.
  • Objective 2: To establish an objective, mathematically rigorous 4-factor scoring rubric (Accuracy 40%, Completeness 25%, Relevance 20%, Ease of Use 15%) evaluated on a 0–100 scale.
  • Objective 3: To isolate and systematically record failure indicators—specifically hallucinations, fabricated citations, and unsafe or outdated assertions—separate from standard usability scores.
  • Objective 4: To engineer a responsive, local-first web platform allowing students to replicate test cases, calculate weighted scores dynamically, perform fact-checking audits, and export academic data without paid API keys.

4. Research Methodology & 4-Factor Scoring

Each AI tool was evaluated across all fifteen test cases using identical standardized prompt instructions and identical ground-truth source assets. Testing was conducted on standard Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge browsers under standard university network conditions during October 2026.

The weighted score calculation is formally governed by the following linear equation:

Weighted Score = (Accuracy × 0.40) + (Completeness × 0.25) + (Relevance × 0.20) + (Ease of Use × 0.15)

The 40% weighting assigned to Accuracy reflects academic ground truth: an AI response that formats elegantly or responds instantaneously but asserts a false mathematical theorem is fundamentally unacceptable for college coursework. Completeness (25%) ensures all sub-questions are answered; Relevance (20%) penalizes tangential filler; and Ease of Use (15%) evaluates formatting clarity, LaTeX equation readability, and copy ergonomics.

6. Fifteen Standardized Test Cases

The evaluation framework encompasses fifteen distinct tasks divided equally across three modalities:

  • IMAGE Tasks (TC-IMG-01 to 05): Logic circuit diagram interpretation, cursive handwritten calculus OCR, biological mitochondria ATP Synthase diagram deconstruction, physics optics bench apparatus detection, and dual-axis macroeconomic chart trend analysis.
  • DOCUMENT Tasks (TC-DOC-01 to 05): 10-page TCP congestion control research paper executive summary, Red-Black tree complexity theorem extraction, ACID transaction citation retrieval, cache coherence MESI study notes, and critical error detection on three planted software engineering falsehoods.
  • AUDIO Tasks (TC-AUD-01 to 05): Verbatim acoustic physics lecture transcription, distributed microservices 15-minute lecture summary, multi-speaker seminar topic taxonomy extraction, technical terminology glossary extraction, and glycolysis study notes cheat sheet formulation.

8. In-Depth Comparative Analysis

A cross-modality comparative analysis reveals profound architectural divergences among the four foundation systems. In the Image Modality, ChatGPT and Claude demonstrated parity in technical precision. In test case TC-IMG-01 (Digital Logic Circuit), Claude identified the Boolean equivalence \(Y = (A \land B) \lor (B \oplus C)\) with impeccable nodal tracking. ChatGPT produced the most elegant truth table formatted in clean LaTeX. Google Gemini demonstrated remarkable visual localization in TC-IMG-04, identifying all five physics lab apparatus with accurate spatial orientations. Copilot, while functional, occasionally misclassified fine millimeter gradations.

In the Document Modality, Claude 3.5 Sonnet emerged as the academic gold standard. In test case TC-DOC-05, which presented a document containing three deliberately planted software lifecycle falsehoods (such as claiming waterfall allows requirement changes during implementation and sprint lengths span 12 months), Claude not only detected all three planted errors with textbook citations from Sommerville's Software Engineering, but additionally critiqued an implicit assumption regarding user acceptance testing. In contrast, Microsoft Copilot caught only two of the three errors, having been influenced by superficial web search results. Google Gemini leveraged its massive 1,000,000-token context window to ingest a ten-page networking PDF instantaneously, retaining subtle tabular testbed throughput figures without truncation.

The Audio Modality represented the single most stark differentiator in the study. On free web interfaces, Google Gemini was the sole platform capable of directly ingesting native audio recordings (.mp3 and .wav files). Gemini transcribed the acoustics Doppler physics lecture (TC-AUD-01) with a Word Error Rate (WER) below 2%, correctly transcribing complex mathematical formulas such as \(\theta = \arcsin(v_{\text{sound}} / v_{\text{source}})\). In contrast, ChatGPT and Claude do not support raw audio file uploads on their free web tiers. While ChatGPT offers an interactive voice mode on mobile applications, desktop students must rely on third-party speech-to-text converters before feeding text into Claude or ChatGPT, introducing substantial workflow friction and penalizing their Ease of Use scores.

9. Limitations & Failure Analysis

Systematic tracking of failure modes revealed critical insights. Two distinct categories of failure were observed:

  1. Web Grounding Contamination: Microsoft Copilot exhibited a tendency to supplement local document queries with external Bing search snippets. While beneficial for general web exploration, this behavior resulted in hallucinated citations in TC-DOC-03, where Copilot cited an external commercial blog rather than the provided course document.
  2. Audio Ingestion Workarounds: Testing audio on tools lacking native file upload required text transcription pipelines or microphone playback, which introduces acoustic ambient noise. Researchers must recognize that free tier capabilities undergo rapid server-side updates by vendors.

10. Student Privacy, Ethics & Safe AI Use

Academic integrity and student data privacy are paramount in generative AI adoption. Public free-tier AI services frequently retain user prompts for model training unless users explicitly configure privacy settings. Consequently, students must adhere to three foundational ethical tenets:

  • Zero Sensitive Data Ingestion: Never upload unredacted student IDs, institutional financial data, or unpublished university examination papers to third-party free AI tools.
  • Independent Verification Mandatory: AI outputs must be treated as preliminary drafts. Students must cross-reference all formulas, historical dates, and biological pathways against official course textbooks.
  • Transparent Citation: When permissible under university academic policies, students must disclose the use of AI tools for formatting, proofreading, or lecture transcription.

11. Conclusion & Student Recommended Workflow

Based on sixty standardized evaluations, no single AI tool dominates every educational domain. Rather, the optimal student strategy leverages a modular, multi-tool workflow:

The Recommended High-Yield Student Stack:
  • For Spoken Lectures & Massive PDFs: Utilize Google Gemini for direct MP3 lecture transcription, speaker summarization, and 100+ page textbook queries.
  • For Critical Document Review & Exam Notes: Utilize Claude 3.5 Sonnet for zero-hallucination paper synthesis, active recall flashcard generation, and rigorous fact-checking.
  • For STEM Problem Solving & Logic Circuits: Utilize ChatGPT for interactive step-by-step math derivations, LaTeX generation, and diagram troubleshooting.
  • For In-Browser PDF Quick Lookup: Utilize Microsoft Copilot in Edge for instant sidebar skimming without manual file uploads.
